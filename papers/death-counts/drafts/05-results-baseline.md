# Baseline Results and Failure Diagnostics

## Baseline Model Results

Tables [-@tbl-seq2seq-baseline] and [-@tbl-llm-baseline] report overall performance for all seq2seq models and LLMs respectively. ConfliBERT-Poisson serves as the baseline model for each set of model evaluations. Values in bold are significantly better than ConfliBERT-Poisson on mean absolute error (MAE), the primary metric, using a paired bootstrap test with 5,000 replications and a threshold of $p < .05$. A dagger indicates that the model is not significantly different from its group's ceiling reference: Flan-T5-XL-QLoRA for the seq2seq models and GPT-4o-mini for the LLMs.

Among the seq2seq models, Flan-T5-XL-QLoRA posts a ceiling MAE of 0.116 and Flan-T5-Large, the primary seq2seq model, achieves 0.156. Both are significantly better than ConfliBERT-Poisson (0.370) on the primary metric, and Flan-T5-Large is not significantly different from the ceiling. T5-Base, IndicBART, mT5-Base, and NT5-Small fall between, with MAEs ranging from 0.272 to 0.334, and none achieves significance over ConfliBERT on overall MAE, though T5-Base, mT5-Base, and NT5-Small do improve significantly on within-1 accuracy. The multilingual pretraining of IndicBART and mT5-Base and the numeracy-augmented objective of NT5-Small yield no consistent advantage over Flan-T5-Large, suggesting that model scale and instruction fine-tuning are the more consequential factors for this task.

Among the LLMs, GPT-4o-mini sets the ceiling at an MAE of 0.133 and is significantly better than ConfliBERT-Poisson across all reported metrics. Llama-3.1-8B, the primary LLM, achieves an MAE of 0.234, and Mistral-7B (0.303) and Mixtral-8×7B (0.297) are comparable to each other and broadly in the range of the weaker seq2seq models. None of the three open-source LLMs achieves significance over ConfliBERT on overall MAE despite substantially lower point estimates, a result attributable to the high variance of absolute errors on rare high-count cases; however, all three do improve significantly on within-1 accuracy and nonzero MAE.

Comparing across both groups, many models improve significantly over ConfliBERT-Poisson on within-1 and within-2 accuracy, reflecting their strong performance on the common low-count bins. The improvement on nonzero MAE reflects a different advantage. Whereas the ConfliBERT-Poisson model underpredicts counts due to the heavy presence of zeros in the training data, the generative models perform more consistently across the full range of nonzero values because they extract counts directly from the text. On the more demanding test of overall MAE, however, only Flan-T5-Large achieves significance among the non-ceiling models in either group. Notably, Flan-T5-Large, Llama-3.1-8B, and Mixtral-8×7B are each statistically indistinguishable from their respective ceiling on MAE, meaning the data cannot rule out that these three models perform as well as the best available in their class. At the ceiling, the results for GPT-4o-mini (MAE 0.133) and Flan-T5-XL-QLoRA (MAE 0.116) are broadly comparable, suggesting that zero-shot prompting of a well-calibrated LLM is competitive with fine-tuning a larger seq2seq model on this task.

## Bin-Level Performance

Figure [-@fig-heatmap-combined] decomposes performance by death count bin, reporting exact match rates for all models across the five bins defined in Section 4. Panel (a) shows the seq2seq models and panel (b) shows the LLMs, each ordered from best to worst by overall MAE, revealing patterns of bin-level accuracy that are harder to detect in aggregate metrics.

- Figure 2 about here - 

Across both panels, all models achieve near-ceiling exact match rates on bins 0, 1, and 2, which together account for roughly 87% of test incidents. However, model performance deteriorates sharply in the rare bins. The ceiling models Flan-T5-XL-QLoRA and GPT-4o-mini reach 84.2% on bin 6+, but this still represents miscoding death counts of about one in six events. The bin-level results of the other models invert the impression from the overall tables, where Flan-T5-Large appeared to hold a clear edge. Flan-T5-Large falls to 86.7% exact match on bin 3-5 and 71.1% on bin 6+, miscoding roughly one in three high-fatality events, and for this bin every LLM outperforms it, with Llama-3.1-8B reaching 78.9% and Mixtral-8×7B matching the ceiling at 84.2%. Meanwhile, ConfliBERT-Poisson collapses to 10.7% on bin 3-5 and 28.9% on bin 6+ due to its dependence on representative examples in the training data.[^power-caveat]

[^power-caveat]: Bin 6+ contains only 38 test observations, yielding confidence intervals of approximately ±8 percentage points for exact match rates. Individual model differences at the bin level are therefore within sampling noise. The overall MAE comparisons, which pool across all test observations, are therefore the more reliable basis for statistical inference.

These patterns reveal how overall performance metrics paint an overly flattering picture by averaging across a distribution heavily weighted toward the common low-count bins. The comparatively high failure rates on bins 3–5 and 6+ are largely invisible in the aggregate figures because such events are less frequently seen in the data. Bin-level decomposition is therefore essential for evaluating models that will be used to construct conflict severity measures, given the potential for masking analytically important counts.

## Diagnosing the Rare-Bin Failures

Next, we closely examine approximately 12 cases that nearly every model has trouble with. These cases suggest how subsequent interventions might improve model performance while revealing a practical limit for any intervention. 

In two cases, the ground-truth label is nonzero but the narrative describes injuries or wounds rather than deaths, and every model returns zero. These are arguably cases where the models are more accurate than the labels [@northcutt2021]. A further two cases involve labels that were updated from follow-up reporting after the original summary was written, so the true count exceeds any number present in the text. No prompt or training intervention can produce a number that is not supported by the narrative, and together these cases set a floor of four irreducible errors on the test set.

However, other cases stemming from the models' tendency to discount unconfirmed attacker deaths reflect a resolvable misalignment between model behavior and the coding protocol. In three to four incidents, security force deaths are confirmed and precise while Maoist casualties are described as "claimed," "suspected," or "bodies taken away by colleagues." Human coders summed both sides, but the models apply an implicit evidentiary discount. The following narrative illustrates this pattern.

> Two CRPF personnel were killed and four others injured during an encounter with cadres of the CPI-Maoist in Giridih District. The encounter followed an ambush by the Maoists on a Police patrol in the Madhuban forest area and it reportedly continued for over 12 hours. Police sources claimed that over a dozen Maoists were killed, but their colleagues managed to take away the bodies. Maoists also triggered seven landmine blasts during the encounter. (true = 14, all models predict 2)

The phrase "managed to take away the bodies" marks a recurring epistemic ambiguity in conflict reporting. The absence of bodies forecloses independent verification of security force claims and we cannot know whether the casualties were genuine, exaggerated or, as documented in the Maoist context, reflective of "fake encounters" and misclassified civilian deaths [@humanrightswatch2008].[^protocol-alignment] These are not straightforwardly model errors since a conservative UCDP-style standard would produce these exact predictions, but they are misaligned with the coding protocol in a way that can be addressed through further instruction and demonstration.

[^protocol-alignment]: The total fatalities variable follows a maximalist coding protocol that includes all reported deaths on all sides, including attacker casualties claimed by security forces even where bodies were not independently confirmed. This is consistent with ACLED-style reporting-based coding but differs from the UCDP lower-bound approach, which discounts single-source security-force claims of combatant deaths. Models prompted without explicit clarification tend to apply an implicit evidentiary discount on claimed casualties, producing counts that are lower than the ground truth but consistent with stricter evidentiary standards.

A final pattern is specific to Flan-T5-Large. In three to four cases where every open-source LLM predicts correctly, Flan-T5-Large overcounts by one or two. All involve narratives that enumerate deaths across multiple victim groups, and the model appears to double-count one group or pick up an extraneous number from context. The following example illustrates the pattern.

> Four CPI-Maoist cadres, a Policeman and two villagers were killed in an encounter between Maoists and Commandos of an anti-Naxal unit, C-60, in a forest of Dhanora in Gadchiroli District. (true = 7, Flan-T5-Large predicts 9)

The LLMs handle these cases correctly, suggesting the failure reflects a capacity limitation rather than a fundamental difficulty with the task. Because the errors follow a consistent structural pattern, targeted training examples, oversampling, and data augmentation strategies that increase the model's exposure to multi-group enumeration cases may help to address them.
