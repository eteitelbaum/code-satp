# Abstract

This paper evaluates the performance of transformer models on the task of extracting death counts from conflict event narratives. Using hand-coded incident summaries from the South Asia Terrorism Portal on the Maoist insurgency in India, I compare fine-tuned sequence-to-sequence models and prompted large language models against a ConfliBERT-Poisson baseline. The distribution of death counts in the corpus is highly skewed, with most incidents concentrated in the lower count values and relatively few observations in the upper tail. I evaluate model performance using overall mean absolute error and a bin-level framework spanning `0`, `1`, `2`, `3-5`, and `6+` deaths. The results show that generative models outperform the encoder baseline on aggregate measures, but that aggregate performance obscures weaker results on rare high-fatality cases. Training-side interventions on Flan-T5-Large yield limited improvement and do not close the gap on the highest fatality bin, whereas prompt-based interventions substantially improve Llama-3.1-8B. Few-shot prompting built around bin-balanced and hard-case examples raises exact match on the `6+` bin from 78.9 percent to 89.5 percent, matching GPT-4o-mini. These findings indicate that automated death count extraction can support conflict research and that its evaluation should account for variation across the count distribution.
