# Abstract

Fatality counts are important outcomes in conflict studies. They determine which events qualify as armed conflict and provide the primary measure of severity, yet automated extraction of count fields has received little systematic evaluation. Using hand-coded incident summaries from the South Asia Terrorism Portal on the Maoist insurgency in India, I compare fine-tuned sequence-to-sequence models and prompted large language models against a ConfliBERT-Poisson baseline. The distribution of death counts in the corpus is highly skewed, with most incidents concentrated in the lower count values and relatively few observations in the upper tail. I evaluate model performance using overall mean absolute error and a bin-level framework spanning `0`, `1`, `2`, `3-5`, and `6+` deaths. The results show that generative models outperform the encoder baseline on aggregate measures, but that aggregate performance obscures weaker results on rare high-fatality cases. Training-side interventions on Flan-T5-Large yield limited improvement and do not close the gap on the highest fatality bin, whereas prompt-based interventions substantially improve Llama-3.1-8B. Few-shot prompting built around bin-balanced and hard-case examples raises exact match on the `6+` bin from 78.9 percent to 89.5 percent, matching GPT-4o-mini. Automated count extraction can reliably support conflict research on low-fatality incidents, but high-fatality cases warrant closer scrutiny.
